TURN 1 · SOMEONE ELSE'S SCRAPBOOK, AS A VISITOR SEES IT
seven ways to organise a page
Same twelve moments from four friends in every frame. Items, flips and wobble are untouched; only arrangement, containers and surfaces change. Every frame is live: scroll, swipe, drag and tap.
STEP 1A · THE COLLAGE ALGORITHM (10 REFERENCES)
Anchor + satellites. One hero per spread at 1.6–2.2× the supporting pieces, set off-centre (upper-left third most often). 3–6 satellites cluster within one hero-width of it.
Overlap chains. Pieces overlap 10–25% of the smaller one and every piece touches at least one other. Overlaps avoid faces and text.
Rotation budget. Most pieces sit at ±2–6°. One loud piece per spread goes to 8–15°. Blocks of writing stay close to 0°.
Hidden columns. Pieces snap to two invisible verticals, then get nudged 4–12px off them. It reads as order without a visible grid.
Whitespace. One large empty region per spread, usually opposite the hero, plus ~8% margins. Gaps between clusters are wider than gaps inside them.
Hierarchy comes from size first, then one high-contrast element (black or a single saturated colour), then an annotation pointing at it.
STEP 1B · MATERIALS, TYPE, CONTAINERS
Materials. Torn paper with a white fibre edge (newsprint, lined, kraft, script), masking tape at 30–45°, bulldog and binder clips on top edges, dot, grid and lined grounds, kraft envelopes, stamps and circular postmarks, label tape, tickets and typed forms.
Type and annotation. Block-caps handwriting for prompts (READ THIS), small-caps sans for form fields (DATE, ISSUED TO, ITEM / DESCRIPTION), loose script for marginalia, arrows and circles linking a note to an object, strike-throughs and a FOUND stamp as corrections.
Containers. Library card pocket, inventory sheet, passport spread with fields and a stamp grid, magazine spreads, journal strata, a folder per collection, diary spreads with arrows.
What carries over. Torn strata and the three paper grounds are the strongest match for your line work. Form fields solve the censored "from": a redacted field reads as deliberate rather than missing.
STEP 1C · MOTION VOCABULARY → WHAT IT'S GOOD AT
Half-dial scrub
Choosing a point on a continuum. Browsing by time.
Curved wheel list
Picking one of many while seeing its neighbours.
Scroll-driven cascade
Sequential story, revealing depth as you go.
Swipe deck + knob
One at a time, full attention. Savouring.
Fanned wallet
Picking one of many while the whole set stays visible.
Freeform canvas
Overview and spatial memory. Weak at time.
Album-crate flick
Fast browsing of many, identified by cover.
3D book of spreads
Chapters, a sense of volume, comparing spreads.
Pinch / rotate / tilt
Inspecting one object closely. Adds depth, not structure.

torn strata
Each month is a strip of torn paper laid over the one before.

When it was dropped. One month per strip, newest on top, like sediment: the page grows as people keep thinking of you. Inside a strip, one hero and one or two satellites.

TEXTURE

4 → 80 ITEMS

BOLD · RANK 6
the pile
Everything in one messy heap on the desk. Flick the top one away to see the next.
PRINCIPLE · STACK, NEWEST ON TOP
Recency, one at a time. Each moment was given on its own, so you take each one on its own.

ITEMS
All types at about 1.1×, centred, the top six visible, each up to ±8° and 12px off-centre.
TEXTURE
Kraft desk, label tape for the date, tape and clips on some items.
4 → 80 ITEMS

the year dial
Turn a dial through the year; each month's moments come up above it.
PRINCIPLE · TIME, SCRUBBED
The selected month decides what's on the page. Filled dots mark months with moments, hollow dots mark quiet ones, so the rhythm of the year shows without numbers.

ITEMS
1–3 per month, hand-placed and larger than in the other directions (0.85–1.0×).
TEXTURE
Dot-grid paper. The dial body is a soft UI component; ticks and labels are drawn by hand; red needle.
4 → 80 ITEMS
4: mostly hollow dots and "a quiet month", which reads as waiting rather than empty. 80: months with more than four items show a small pile; the dial gets one ring per year.
